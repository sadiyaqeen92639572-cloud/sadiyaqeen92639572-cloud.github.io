A1 vs A2 microSD: Which Do You Actually Need?

App Performance Class (A1/A2) measures random I/O speed — not sequential read/write:

  • A1 — 1,500 IOPS random read: Enough for Linux-based handhelds (Miyoo, single-slot Anbernic, Powkiddy). ROMs load sequentially and hit bus limits before class matters.
  • A2 — 4,000 IOPS random read: Required for Android handhelds (Retroid Pocket 4 Pro, Odin 2). Android apps depend on random I/O. Also recommended for TF1 OS cards on dual-slot Anbernic.

Bottom line: If your handheld runs Linux firmware (OnionOS, GarlicOS, Batocera) → A1 is fine. If it runs Android → A2 is mandatory.

Frequently Asked Questions

What microSD card should I buy for Miyoo Mini Plus?
The Miyoo Mini Plus has a hard 256GB limit — larger cards are simply not recognized. Community standard: Samsung EVO Select 256GB (A2, ~$18.99). Don't overspend on SanDisk Extreme for this console — A2 vs A1 makes no difference on OnionOS.
Do I need A2 for Anbernic RG35XX or RG35XX Plus?
No. Single-slot Anbernic consoles running GarlicOS or Batocera load ROMs sequentially — both A1 and A2 cards hit the bus ceiling before class makes a difference. Save money with an A1 card (Kingston Canvas Select+, Kioxia Exceria) and spend the difference on more capacity.
What cards work best for TF1 OS slot on Anbernic dual-slot consoles?
The TF1 slot holds your OS firmware and writes logs constantly — endurance matters here. Best picks: Samsung Pro Endurance 32–64GB or SanDisk Max Endurance 32–64GB. Keep TF1 small (32GB is ideal) and use a large, cheap card for TF2 ROMs.
Should I throw away the microSD card that came with my Anbernic?
Yes, immediately. OEM cards bundled with Anbernic, Powkiddy, and Trimui devices are consistently low-quality. r/sbcgaming sees multiple data corruption reports per week from bundled cards. Replace with Samsung, SanDisk, or Lexar on day one.
What is the best microSD for Retroid Pocket 4 Pro?
Retroid Pocket 4 Pro requires A2 class — Android depends on random I/O for UI smoothness and game loading. Minimum: Samsung EVO Select 512GB (A2, ~$34.99). If your collection includes PS2 and GameCube, 512GB is the practical minimum. The RP4 Pro supports 1TB+ cards.
How often do microSD prices change on Amazon?
Frequently — especially Samsung and SanDisk which run Lightning Deals weekly. $/GB can drop 20–35% during a sale. The static prices on this page are reference points only. Check bestmicrosd.com for prices updated every Monday from live Amazon data.
Does MB/s read speed matter for retro gaming?
Almost not at all. ROM files load sequentially and the SDIO bus in retro handhelds is typically saturated long before you hit the card's rated speed. A 150 MB/s card and a 190 MB/s card will feel identical loading the same ROM. Prioritize $/GB and App Class (A2 for Android) over raw MB/s.
