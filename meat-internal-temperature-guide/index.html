Why Internal Temperature Is the Only Reliable Doneness Test

Colour, juices and cooking time are unreliable indicators of doneness. A chicken can appear fully cooked while the centre is still below the safe temperature. A thick beef joint may look pink at 71°C well-done. Only a calibrated thermometer gives a definitive answer.

The UK Food Standards Agency (FSA) and USDA both define food safety by internal temperature — not time, not colour, not whether juices run clear. A chicken thigh showing clear juices but reading 68°C is not safe; one showing slightly pink juices at 74°C is.

Where to Insert the Thermometer

  • Roasting joints: deepest part of the thickest section, parallel to the bone but not touching it
  • Whole chicken: inner thigh, near the breast — the last part to reach temperature
  • Thick steaks: horizontal from the side, tip centred in the meat
  • Rolled joints: through the side to the geometric centre

Carryover Cooking — Why You Pull Early

After removing meat from the oven, residual heat continues raising the internal temperature by 2–5°C over 5–15 minutes. For medium-rare beef at a target of 57°C, pull the joint at 52–54°C and rest for 10 minutes. This is not optional — it's how professional kitchens achieve consistent results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the safe internal temperature for chicken? ▼

74°C (165°F) for all poultry. Check the inner thigh, away from the bone — it's the last part to reach temperature. At 74°C, Salmonella and Campylobacter are fully destroyed.

What internal temperature is medium-rare beef? ▼

57–60°C (135–140°F). Remove from the oven at 52–54°C and rest for 10 minutes — carryover heat will raise it to the target. The centre will be pink, warm and slightly firm.

What temperature should pork be cooked to in the UK? ▼

The FSA recommends 75°C for pork to be safe. The USDA reduced their minimum to 63°C with a 3-minute rest in 2011. For pulled pork (shoulder), cook to 90–95°C to break down collagen for that tender texture.

Does internal temperature rise after removing from the oven? ▼

Yes — by 2–5°C for most joints. This is called carryover cooking. Always pull the meat 3–5°C below your target temperature and allow 10–20 minutes rest for large roasts. The temperature will equalise across the joint during resting.

Where do you put a meat thermometer in a chicken? ▼

Insert the probe into the thickest part of the inner thigh, close to but not touching the bone. Do not check the breast — it reaches temperature faster and will give a falsely reassuring reading. The thigh must reach 74°C before the bird is safe.

What temperature is medium lamb? ▼

Medium lamb is 63°C (145°F). Rare lamb is 52°C, medium-rare 57°C, and well done 71°C. For a leg of lamb, most UK recipes target medium (63°C) with a 15–20 minute rest before carving.

Can I use a cheap dial thermometer for meat? ▼

Dial (bimetal) thermometers are accurate but slow — allow 15–20 seconds to stabilise. They should be calibrated regularly in ice water (0°C / 32°F). Digital instant-read thermometers are faster (2–5 seconds) and generally more accurate for oven use.
