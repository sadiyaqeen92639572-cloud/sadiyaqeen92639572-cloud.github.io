How Overtime Pay Works — Step by Step

1. Confirm you're non-exempt

Overtime rules under the FLSA apply to "non-exempt" employees. Salaried "exempt" employees (executives, administrators, and professionals meeting minimum salary thresholds) generally aren't entitled to overtime. Hourly employees are almost always non-exempt.

2. Count hours over 40/week

Federal overtime is based on a 7-day workweek total, not daily hours. Only hours worked beyond 40 in that single workweek count as overtime, unless your state has its own daily-overtime rule (see table above).

3. Apply the multiplier

Multiply your regular hourly rate by 1.5 (or 2 for double-time, where it applies) to get your overtime rate, then multiply by overtime hours worked.

4. Add to regular pay

Total gross pay = (regular hours × regular rate) + (overtime hours × overtime rate). This is your gross pay before any tax withholding.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is overtime pay calculated?
Overtime pay is typically your regular hourly rate × 1.5 (time-and-a-half) for hours worked over 40 in a workweek, under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). For example, at $20/hour, overtime pay is $30/hour. Multiply that by your overtime hours to get your overtime earnings, then add your regular 40-hour pay for total gross pay.
What is the difference between time-and-a-half and double-time?
Time-and-a-half is 1.5x your regular hourly rate — the federal FLSA minimum for hours over 40/week. Double-time (2x rate) is not required by federal law but is common in union contracts, some state laws (e.g. California after 12 hours/day or on the 7th consecutive workday), and some employer policies for holidays.
Does every state require overtime after 8 hours in a day?
No — federal FLSA overtime is based on a 40-hour workweek, not daily hours. California, Alaska, and a few other states have their own daily-overtime rules (e.g. California requires 1.5x after 8 hours/day and 2x after 12 hours/day). Check your state labor department if you regularly work long single-day shifts.
Are salaried employees entitled to overtime pay?
It depends on whether you're classified as "exempt" or "non-exempt" under the FLSA. Non-exempt salaried employees are still entitled to overtime. Exempt employees (typically salaried professionals, executives, and administrators meeting minimum salary thresholds) are not. Misclassification is common — check with your state labor department if you're unsure.
Is overtime pay taxed differently than regular pay?
No — overtime pay is taxed the same as regular wages for federal income tax, FICA, and state income tax purposes. It's added to your gross pay for that pay period, which can push you into a higher withholding bracket for that paycheck, but it isn't taxed at a special "overtime rate."
Does this calculator include tax withholding?
No — this calculator shows gross overtime pay only (before taxes). For your actual take-home pay after federal tax, FICA, and your state's income tax, use a full paycheck calculator that covers your specific state.
How many overtime hours can my employer require?
Federal law does not cap the number of overtime hours an adult employee can be required to work — it only requires that hours over 40/week be paid at 1.5x. Some states restrict mandatory overtime for specific professions (e.g. healthcare workers). There's no federal limit on total hours worked per week for most adult non-exempt employees.
