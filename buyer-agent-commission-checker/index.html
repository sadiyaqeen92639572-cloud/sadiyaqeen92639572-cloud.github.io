How to Check Who Owes What — Step by Step

1. Know your sale price

The buyer-agent fee is calculated as a percentage of the home's sale price — typically in the 2.5%-3% range per side, though this varies by market and is now fully negotiable.

2. Pick the real coverage scenario

Ask directly: is the seller covering the buyer's agent in full, not at all, or as a partial concession negotiated into the offer? This is now a specific written agreement, not an MLS default.

3. Read the breakdown

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the seller still pay the buyer's agent commission in 2026?
Not automatically. Effective August 17, 2024, the NAR settlement removed buyer-agent compensation offers from MLS listings. The seller can still agree to cover it as a negotiated concession, but it's no longer the default — buyer and buyer's agent now sign a written agreement covering the buyer's agent's fee before touring homes.
What is the NAR settlement and when did it take effect?
The National Association of Realtors settled a major antitrust lawsuit over commission-sharing rules. As part of the settlement, effective August 17, 2024, MLS listings can no longer include buyer-agent compensation offers, and buyer's agents must have a signed written agreement with their buyer before showing homes.
How many sellers still cover the buyer's agent fee?
Survey data (Clever Real Estate, 2026) suggests roughly 35% of sellers still choose to cover some or all of the buyer's agent's fee as a negotiating concession — it's now optional and case-by-case rather than standard practice.
If the seller doesn't pay, who pays the buyer's agent?
The buyer does, directly, per their signed buyer-agency agreement — unless it's negotiated as a seller concession during the offer process, or the buyer rolls it into their financing where their loan program allows it.
Can buyer-agent commission still be negotiated into the offer?
Yes. Buyers can ask sellers to cover some or all of their agent's fee as part of the purchase offer — it's now an explicit negotiation point rather than an assumed MLS-listed amount.
Does this checker give exact numbers for my sale?
No — this is a quick directional estimate using a typical commission range. For a full breakdown including listing-agent split, net proceeds, and closing costs, use a dedicated real estate commission calculator with the buyer-agent-coverage toggle built in.
