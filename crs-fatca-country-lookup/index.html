Why Check a Country's CRS/FATCA Status

Whether a bank reports your account information — and to whom — depends first on which international reporting regimes the account's country participates in. CRS (Common Reporting Standard) and FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) are the two frameworks that determine this. A country can be CRS-committed, have a FATCA IGA (Model 1 or Model 2), both, or neither.

This Tells You About the Country. It Doesn't Tell You About You.

Knowing a country's CRS/FATCA status is half the picture. Whether you personally are reportable also depends on your citizenship, your tax residency, and whether you're a US person — three factors this lookup doesn't ask about.

Here's when that distinction actually matters:

  • US citizens abroad: FATCA applies to you wherever you bank, regardless of the account country's CRS status — a fact this lookup alone won't surface.
  • Dual tax residents: If you have ties to two countries, which one your bank actually reports to depends on a tax-treaty tie-breaker, not just a country lookup.
  • Opening a new account: Before choosing a jurisdiction, you want to know your own resulting obligation, not just the country's general status.
  • Confirming your bank's self-certification form: Banks ask you to certify your own FATCA/CRS status — getting it wrong on the form causes real problems later.
  • Green card holders and long-term US residents: FATCA can apply even without US citizenship — a case a bare country lookup can't catch.

The FATCA/CRS Reportability Tool asks the 5 questions that actually determine your personal status — citizenship, green card, US day-count, tax residency, and account country — and gives you a direct, plain-language answer plus the exact self-certification form you'll need.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CRS?
CRS (Common Reporting Standard) is the OECD's global standard for automatic exchange of financial account information. Banks in CRS-participating countries report account holder identity and balances to the account holder's country of tax residence every year, if that country also participates in CRS.
What is a FATCA IGA?
A FATCA intergovernmental agreement (IGA) is a treaty between the US and another country implementing FATCA. Model 1 IGA countries have banks report to their own local tax authority, which forwards the data to the IRS. Model 2 IGA countries have banks report directly to the IRS.
Does the United States participate in CRS?
No. The United States does not participate in CRS — it uses FATCA instead, its own unilateral reporting regime. This is a common point of confusion: being a US person doesn't trigger CRS, it triggers FATCA.
How often is this data updated?
Quarterly, via an automated refresh directly from the OECD's AEOI status-of-commitments document and the US Treasury's FATCA agreements table — the same two primary sources used by regulators.
My country isn't listed as CRS-committed — does that mean no reporting happens?
Not necessarily. A jurisdiction not yet committed to a CRS exchange date may still have other domestic reporting obligations, or may be in the process of joining. This lookup reflects the OECD's own committed-jurisdictions list, not a complete picture of every possible reporting mechanism.
What's the difference between Model 1 and Model 2 FATCA IGA?
Model 1: the bank reports to its own country's tax authority, which forwards the data to the IRS. Model 2: the bank reports directly to the IRS, without going through a local authority first. Most FATCA partner countries use Model 1.
