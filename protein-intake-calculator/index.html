Why Body Weight Alone Doesn't Tell the Whole Story

Two people at the same body weight can have very different protein needs depending on activity level, muscle mass, and whether they're trying to gain, maintain, or lose weight. That's why this calculator asks for a goal, not just a weight — a sedentary person and a person lifting weights four times a week at the same bodyweight can have gram targets more than double apart.

Cutting/fat loss targets skew higher than muscle-building targets, which can seem counterintuitive — the reasoning documented in the research is that adequate protein helps preserve lean muscle mass while eating in a calorie deficit, so the body pulls less from muscle tissue for energy.

Spreading Protein Across the Day

  • 3-4 meals is a commonly cited sweet spot rather than one or two large protein hits
  • 20-40g per meal is often enough to support muscle protein synthesis per sitting
  • Post-workout window is commonly prioritized, though total daily intake matters more than exact timing

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein do I need per day? ▼

The U.S. RDA, set by the Food and Nutrition Board, is 0.8g per kg of body weight — a figure that has stayed unchanged for more than 70 years and represents the minimum to avoid nitrogen loss, not an optimal target. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) Position Stand recommends 1.4-2.0g/kg for people who exercise regularly.

Do I need more protein when losing weight? ▼

A peer-reviewed review on very low-calorie diets found 1.2-1.7g/kg effectively maintains fat-free mass and strength during energy restriction for recreational exercisers. The ISSN Position Stand goes further for resistance-trained individuals, citing 2.3-3.1g/kg to maximize lean mass retention during a calorie deficit — this calculator uses 2.2g/kg as a middle-ground target for general (non-elite) cutting.

How much protein do I need to build muscle? ▼

The ISSN Position Stand on Protein and Exercise states 1.4-2.0g per kg of body weight per day is sufficient for building and maintaining muscle mass in most exercising individuals, distributed in doses of roughly 20-40g every 3-4 hours.

Is it bad to eat too much protein? ▼

The ISSN Position Stand notes evidence that even intakes above 3.0g/kg/day have shown positive body-composition effects in resistance-trained individuals without reported harm in healthy adults. People with existing kidney conditions should follow their doctor's guidance rather than a general calculator.

Should I spread protein across meals or eat it all at once? ▼

The ISSN Position Stand specifically recommends doses of roughly 20-40g of high-quality protein spread every 3-4 hours across the day, rather than concentrating intake into one or two meals.

Does this calculator account for body fat percentage? ▼

No — it uses total body weight, the simpler approach used in the RDA and most general guidelines. Lean-mass-based calculations exist and can be more precise for people with higher body fat percentages, but require a body fat measurement this tool doesn't ask for.

What foods have the most protein per calorie? ▼

Whole foods like cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, chicken breast, egg whites, and white fish tend to offer the most protein per calorie compared to fattier meats or processed protein snacks.
